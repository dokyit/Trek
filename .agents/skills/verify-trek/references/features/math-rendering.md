# Math rendering

## What it does

Typesets the TeX math agents write in their answers, natively (no web view, no images): `$…$` and `\(…\)` sit in the line of text on its baseline, `$$…$$` and `\[…\]` are centred on a line of their own and scroll sideways when wider than the column. Fractions stack with a rule, scripts are smaller and offset, roots get a radical, `\left…\right` delimiters grow with their content, and matrices, `aligned` and `cases` environments line up. Glyphs are the KaTeX fonts Trek embeds, drawn in the text's own colour, so both themes and every text size work. It applies everywhere answers render: thread answers and thoughts, the side chat, and the Notes preview.

Dollars that are money or shell (`costs $5 and $10`, `$HOME`, `$(pwd)`) stay prose; code spans and fenced code are never math; `\$` is a dollar. A formula whose closing delimiter hasn't arrived yet shows as plain text until it closes. An unknown command shows its name rather than vanishing. Copying the answer (the turn's Copy button, or selecting across a formula) gives the original TeX.

## Reach it as a user

Ask an agent a maths, probability or statistics question; it is told that TeX renders and answers in it.

## Reach it with the CLI

```sh
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev open draft
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev send "mock:math"
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev screenshot math-night --output /tmp/trek-math-night.png
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control theme paper
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev screenshot math-paper --output /tmp/trek-math-paper.png
```

`mock:math stream` sends the same answer at reading pace, to watch formulas arrive (an open `\[` shows as text, then typesets once its `\]` lands). The answer is deterministic: Bayes' rule with a `\boxed` result, inline math with `\(…\)` and `$…$`, a `$$` block, a display formula inside a list item, an `aligned` block beside a `pmatrix` and `cases`, `\binom` and `\sqrt[3]`, and a line of money and shell dollars that must stay as written. Its thought carries inline math too.

## Code

Detection and masking (before Markdown reads the text): `crates/trek-app/src/math/detect.rs`. TeX parsing: `math/parse.rs`. Layout by TeX's rules with KaTeX's font metrics: `math/layout.rs`, `math/tables.rs` (generated by `script/math-tables.py`). Drawing and the Markdown plugins: `math/mod.rs`, registered in `md.rs` (`dress`). The source-mask hook and the paragraph split for display math in running text: `vendor/gpui-base/src/text/markdown_ext.rs` and `format/markdown.rs` ("Trek:"). Fonts: `crates/trek-app/assets/fonts/KaTeX_*.ttf` and `TrekMath_*.ttf` (the KaTeX faces without an `m`, which GPUI refuses to load, given one and renamed by `script/math-fonts.py`), registered in `fonts.rs`; a log line "has no 'm' character and was not loaded" means a face is missing. Everything is drawn with glyphs and quads, never paths: GPUI's path pipeline stalled the GPU (the app froze on the next frame) with this many paths in a transcript. The agents' note: `AGENT_INSTRUCTIONS` in `crates/trek-core/src/visualization.rs`. Mock answer: `MATH` in `crates/trek-agents/src/mock.rs`.

## Verify

In both themes: the Bayes fraction is stacked with its bar on the maths axis and the boxed result framed; Greek letters and variables are italic, digits and operators upright; `\mid`, `\approx`, `\le` and `\ne` are proper symbols with relation spacing; `\sum` and `\lim` take limits above and below in display; the `pmatrix` parentheses span both rows; inline formulas sit on the text baseline without pushing lines far apart. "costs $5 and $10" and `echo $HOME` read exactly as written. No raw `\frac`, `\[` or `[` remains. `logs --grep error` is empty.
