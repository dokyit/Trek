# iPhone tool

## What it does

Shows the user's real iPhone, plugged into the Mac by USB (or over Wi-Fi with no cable, below), beside the chat: its screen live over the cable, taps and keys from the user or an agent (`trek-mcp phone`), and apps and files over usbmuxd. The screen, apps and files need no Xcode, Developer Mode or app on the phone; the Trek Driver route below needs all three (it's for a Mac that builds Trek's iPhone app).

Taps take one of three routes, decided from what Trek knows (`Facts::route`, `crates/trek-phone/src/route.rs`):

- **USB (Trek Driver)**: while the iPhone is unlocked on the cable and Trek's iPhone app was built and installed from this Mac with `ios/scripts/install-device.sh` (which now builds Trek Driver into it). The driver (`ios/TrekDriver`) is a small XCUITest that serves HTTP on the phone's loopback; Trek starts it on the first tap with `xcodebuild test-without-building` on the `.xctestrun` the script left in `~/Library/Application Support/dev.trek.phone-update/driver/` (about 7 s warm; longer the first time, when xcodebuild installs the app), reaches its port 8117 through its own usbmuxd client over the cable (over Wi-Fi straight over TCP at the phone's address, which usbmuxd doesn't carry; the driver then takes only this Mac's connections, and an answer counts as whole by its Content-Length so a late close over Wi-Fi doesn't cut it short), and stops it at `phone_release`, when the tool closes, or after 10 idle minutes (the driver also stops itself after 15). Each start gets a fresh token the driver checks. Taps are ~0.3 s, a swipe its length plus ~0.4 s. The driver lives inside Trek's iPhone app rather than its own runner app because a free Apple ID installs at most three apps on a phone (the test bundle goes into `Trek.app/PlugIns`, the app gets the `continuous` background mode, both re-signed; `ios/scripts/build-driver.sh`). A locked phone goes to iPhone Mirroring.

- **Wi-Fi (Trek Driver, no cable)**: the same driver route when usbmuxd sees the phone over the network only. The picture comes from the driver too (nothing comes over a cable), apps and files go over Wi-Fi through usbmuxd's network entry, and the header says Wi-Fi. What it needs, once:
  1. The iPhone plugged in and trusted once (Trust This Computer), so the Mac holds its pairing record.
  2. **Show this iPhone when on Wi-Fi**: in Finder, with the iPhone plugged in, select it in the sidebar, General › Options › Show this iPhone when on Wi-Fi, Apply (stored on the phone as lockdown's `EnableWifiConnections`). usbmuxd then lists it on the network whenever both are on the same Wi-Fi.
  3. **Xcode reaches it over the network**: paired with Xcode by cable once (Developer Mode on, as for the USB route). Xcode 15 and later then reach it over Wi-Fi by themselves; Window › Devices and Simulators shows it with a network icon when unplugged (tick **Connect via network** there if your Xcode shows that box). Check: `xcrun xcdevice list` says `"interface" : "network"` and `xcrun devicectl list devices` says `available (paired)`.
  4. Trek Driver built from this source or later (`ios/scripts/install-device.sh`, or the phone updater's next install): `build-driver.sh` writes `wifi` in the driver's `features`; an older driver says "Install the driver" over Wi-Fi and keeps working on the cable.
  5. The iPhone unlocked. Trek asks CoreDevice every 8 s while the tool is open (`xcrun devicectl device info lockState`, ~1.5 s): `passcodeRequired` true (locked) sends the route to iPhone Mirroring (or Bluetooth), as on the cable. A screen that just went dark within the passcode's grace period counts as unlocked.

  Over Wi-Fi usbmuxd connects to the phone's network address, not its loopback, so Trek starts the driver with `TREK_DRIVER_PEERS` = the Mac's own address toward the phone (`device::local_address_toward`): the driver then listens on the local network too (never cellular, local addresses only) and answers only that address, with the token; anything else gets a 403 naming the address it came from. A driver started over the cable serves the loopback only, so unplugging stops it and the next frame or tap starts it again for Wi-Fi; one started over Wi-Fi keeps answering when the cable comes back. The cable is preferred whenever it's plugged in. Trek's iPhone app already asks for the local network (`NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`); if the iPhone asks, allow it. Starting over Wi-Fi is slower than over the cable, and so are the frames.

- **Bluetooth**: the phone paired once with Trek as a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse (an iPhone on another Apple Account). AssistiveTouch is turned on while Trek taps and off after.
- **iPhone Mirroring**: an iPhone on the Mac's own Apple Account can't take Trek as a keyboard, so taps go through macOS's iPhone Mirroring window in the background. It connects only while the iPhone is locked; Trek opens it behind the user's windows and presses Connect. Trek takes this route once iPhone Mirroring has shown an iPhone (remembered in `<data>/phone-mirroring`, with the name it showed), or when Bluetooth can't work.

## Reach it as a user

Press ⌘J and choose **iPhone**. The header is one line (the device, its name, iOS and the link). Under it one strip: **Screen** (Live, Live · Wi-Fi, Live in iPhone Mirroring, Locked, None over Wi-Fi, …) and **Taps** (Ready · USB, Ready · Wi-Fi, Starting the driver…, Install the driver (ios/scripts/install-device.sh), The driver didn't start, Ready · Bluetooth, Ready · iPhone Mirroring, Lock the iPhone to tap, Unlock the iPhone to tap, Pair once in Bluetooth, iPhone Mirroring can't reach it, …), with **AssistiveTouch** only on the Bluetooth route. A line under the strip appears only when the user must act now; the ⓘ button opens "How this works" (its "Over Wi-Fi" paragraph comes second while there's no cable, last otherwise).

Over Wi-Fi, locked: when iPhone Mirroring is ready it takes the taps with nothing to do; when it can't (it timed out, isn't on this Mac, or Bluetooth isn't paired) the line says "Unlock the iPhone to let Trek tap it over Wi-Fi." Unlocked with a driver that can't run (not installed, or Xcode can't reach the phone) and iPhone Mirroring known here, the line adds "or lock the iPhone to tap through iPhone Mirroring".

The user's own mouse, trackpad and keys work on the picture as a finger and a keyboard would (`crates/trek-app/src/panels/phone/touch.rs`, the gestures in `crates/trek-phone/src/gesture.rs`): a click is a tap held as long as the button was (hold half a second or more for a long press: the dot under the pointer turns sky-ringed); a drag is recorded with its timing, a rest before the first move included, and played on the phone when the button comes up (a trail follows the pointer meanwhile); trackpad or wheel scrolling over the picture becomes swipes that move the content as far, the same way, kept clear of the screen's edges (sent when the scroll pauses); a click on the picture gives it the keys ("Typing goes to the iPhone · esc" under the strip, the bezel ringed): text goes with `/type`, Return, Delete and Tab with `/key`, the arrows too from a driver with `keys`, ⌘V types the Mac's clipboard, ⌘-shortcuts stay Trek's, and Escape goes to the phone and gives the keys back (as does a click outside the tool). Over Trek Driver the user's inputs go one at a time in order, text typed meanwhile merged into one `/type`. A driver from before `/path` (version 1, what the phone updater last installed) gets each drag as one straight `/swipe` over its moving part and no arrows; holds and scrolls work the same. Bluetooth plays the whole path and holds too; iPhone Mirroring holds taps and drags straight. The user's input wins over an agent holding the phone: the agent loses it as with Stop.

While the picture comes over the cable, iOS draws 9:41 and full bars in its status bar, and the Calendar icon's demo date, on the phone itself too (as for QuickTime). So on the USB route the panel takes its picture from Trek Driver instead whenever the driver can run (the phone unlocked on the cable, the driver installed) and somebody looks (the panel on screen, or an agent holding the phone): `manage_driver_frames` starts the driver if need be and the driver pushes its frames (`GET /stream`, below); the first frame switches the picture over and turns the cable's capture off, and the phone shows its real clock again within a few seconds of the tool opening. A driver without `stream` in its `features` (version 2, until the phone updater replaces it) is asked for a JPEG about three times a second instead, as before (`GET /screenshot?w=900&q=70`, every 110 ms for 3 s after the user's input). An agent's `phone_screenshot` comes from the driver too then (a PNG scaled to `max_edge`, taken by itself, never a frame of the stream). When the driver stops (the phone locked or unplugged) or nobody looks (the panel hidden and no agent holding the phone), the stream is closed and the cable's capture takes over again.

**The stream.** `GET /stream?w=&q=&fps=` is answered with `multipart/x-mixed-replace; boundary=trekframe` for as long as the Mac keeps the connection: each part `--trekframe`, `Content-Type: image/jpeg`, `Content-Length`, a blank line, the JPEG, a line break. One loop on a thread of its own in the driver (`ios/TrekDriver/Driver/Stream.swift`) takes the screen at most `fps` times a second and sends a frame only when it differs from the last one taken; a still screen is looked at four times a second (a tap or key through the driver, or a change, brings the full pace back at once), and a watcher that has heard nothing for a second gets an empty part (`Content-Length: 0`), so a dead connection and a still screen don't look alike. Nothing is taken while nobody watches, at most two watch (a third gets 503 and falls back to screenshots for 10 s), and a slow watcher is owed only the newest frame. The screen is asked of XCTest's daemon as a JPEG directly (`Capture.daemonJPEG`, the technique of WebDriverAgent's MJPEG server), not through `XCUIScreen.screenshot()`: 0.02–0.03 s against 0.32 s a frame in the iOS 27 simulator, and 0.08 s against 0.8 s while a gesture plays; three failures in a row fall back to the public screenshot for the rest of the run (`/status` says which under `stream.via`). `w` is reached by halving the picture, which ImageIO does in about 20 ms (to exactly 900 pixels took 85): over the cable Trek asks for the screen's own size (`w=0&q=70&fps=30`), over Wi-Fi for `w=900&q=60&fps=24`, which is half size while the screen moves and one frame at full size when it comes to rest. On the Mac `Driver::watch` reads it (`driver::Mjpeg`, `device::stream`: through usbmuxd on the cable, plain TCP over Wi-Fi), and when the connection drops (the phone locked, the cable out, the Wi-Fi address changed) the loop checks the driver, starts it again if it went, and connects again after 0.25 s, then 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 s (`driver::after_stream`). Gestures never wait for it: a tap took 0.30 s with nobody watching and with two.

Measured in the iOS 27 simulator (iPhone 17) with `cargo run -p trek-phone --example driver_bench -- 127.0.0.1:<port> <token>`, three runs each way: while a finger drags the Home Screen's pages about, screenshots one at a time gave 1.4 to 2.8 new frames a second and the stream 22 to 28; from a swipe being sent to the first frame showing it, 0.55 to 1.4 s (median) against 0.11 to 0.25 s (0.11 to 0.14 after a first run in which one swipe of six took 2 s to show). Not measured on a real iPhone.

**Elements for agents.** `phone_elements` reads the screen as the outline computer use's `elements` and `sim_elements` give (one format and one implementation, `crates/trek-mcp/src/outline.rs`), `phone_tap` takes a `ref` from it, and `phone_wait` waits for text or an element to show or go. The driver's `GET /elements?max=&ms=` (`ios/TrekDriver/Driver/Elements.swift`) snapshots each app XCTest counts as active (the app in front; SpringBoard on the Home Screen, and SpringBoard alone under a system alert such as a permission prompt) and flattens it: `{"app", "width", "height", "elements": [{"type", "label", "identifier", "value", "frame": [x, y, w, h] in points, "enabled", "selected", "focused", "depth"}], "more", "partial", "ms"}`. A secure field has `"secure": true` and never a `value`. Listed are the elements on screen that say something or are controls; containers and pictures that say nothing, the status bar, a keyboard's keys and repeats are left out. It is bounded: `max` elements (default 300, the rest counted in `more`), and `ms` (default 5000) both for walking the tree (`partial` when it ran out) and for XCTest's own snapshot, whose 60 s limit is lowered for the call. Trek answers `phone_elements` with that list plus `picture`, the size of the picture x/y are pixels of (the latest `phone_screenshot`'s, or before any the size the next one will have); `trek-mcp` (`crates/trek-mcp/src/phone.rs`, `Screen::parse`) reads the list into the shared outline's tree, dropping a control's own text listed again inside it and the scroll indicators, and the shared code writes it, gives out the refs and checks them. The answer starts `Elements of the iPhone's screen. frame=x,y,width,height in phone_screenshot pixels (552x1200).`, then `App: Settings (com.apple.Preferences)` (the app in front by name and bundle id; SpringBoard for the Home Screen or a system alert) and `Focused: …` when something has the keyboard, then a line per element: `- button "General" [ref=e2] [id=com.apple.settings.general] [frame=22,522,508,71]`. Roles are in plain words (`text field`, `navigation bar`), state is flags (`[disabled]`, `[selected]`, `[focused]`; a switch's 1 or 0 is `[checked]` or `[unchecked]`), `[id=…]` is the accessibility identifier, a secure field is `[secure]` and never has a value, and frames are in the picture's pixels (the picture is the screen scaled to its width). `filter` (role, name or id), `interactive_only` and `max` (150 by default; the driver is asked for at most 400) narrow or widen it. What can be tapped (anything but a container) gets a ref, kept from one reading to the next: an element is the same one when its app, the containers it sits in, its type, identifier and label are and nothing else on the screen says the same (that stands in for an identity, which the driver doesn't give), so it keeps its ref wherever it goes; those that say the same as another keep theirs by their place among the same neighbours. A tap by ref reads the screen again and refuses, with "Read the elements again (phone_elements)", when the element isn't at its place in the tree any more, another one is there, it moved by more than about half its size, or what's around one of several alike changed; a disabled one is refused too. Otherwise it taps its centre (of the part on screen) through the ordinary `phone_tap` x/y. `phone_wait` with a `ref` waits on the element wherever on the screen it is; with `text`, on a name, value or identifier. On the Bluetooth and iPhone Mirroring routes, and with a driver from before version 3, these tools say elements need Trek Driver and to use `phone_screenshot` and x/y. A tap, swipe or key that went out to the driver without its answer coming back is reported as "Outcome unknown: …" and never sent again by Trek or trek-mcp (the same words when the Bluetooth helper or iPhone Mirroring didn't answer in time). Over the cable the panel draws the phone's real time (its time zone and 12/24-hour setting, read from lockdown) over the clock, in the status bar's own colors, only when a frame shows a status bar (not over a full-screen app or iPhone Mirroring's picture) and never below it.

## Reach it with the CLI

An isolated run never touches the real phone: the tool starts with a made-up one, and `control phone <state>` puts it in each situation (this also opens the tool).

```sh
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone usb        # unlocked, Mirroring route: Lock the iPhone to tap
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone lock       # the same while someone wants taps: the action line
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone locked     # locked, iPhone Mirroring closed: Ready
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone unreachable  # locked, timed out, diagnosed: the blocker and Check again
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone mirroring  # iPhone Mirroring showing the phone
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone pair       # Bluetooth route, not paired yet
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone bluetooth  # paired: Ready · Bluetooth, AssistiveTouch chip
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone none       # no iPhone on the cable
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone how        # the "How this works" popover
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone driver           # unlocked, driver installed: Ready · USB
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone driver-starting  # Starting the driver…
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone driver-install   # Install the driver (ios/scripts/install-device.sh), with its action line
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone driver-how       # the popover on the USB route
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone driver-live      # the driver's picture: Screen Live, the clock real
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone wifi             # no cable: header Wi-Fi, Screen Live · Wi-Fi, Taps Ready · Wi-Fi
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone wifi-starting    # no cable, the driver starting: Screen Starting…, Taps Starting the driver…
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone wifi-locked      # no cable, locked, iPhone Mirroring can't reach it: Unlock the iPhone to let Trek tap it over Wi-Fi.
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone wifi-how         # the popover with no cable: "Over Wi-Fi" second
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone touch            # a made-up Home Screen, a drag under way: its trail and the touch dot
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone hold             # a press held into a long press: the dot larger, sky-ringed
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone typing           # the picture has the keys: "Typing goes to the iPhone · esc", the bezel ringed
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev screenshot iphone-tool
```

A live check on the real phone (it starts a second helper beside the user's Trek's; switch back to a made-up state when done):

```sh
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone real
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone 'call phone_status {}'
cat "$(./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev status | jq -r .runtime_dir)/shot/phone-reply.json"
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone 'call phone_release {}'
./.agents/skills/verify-trek/scripts/trek-dev control phone usb
```

`call <method> <json>` runs any `phone_*` tool as an agent on full access would; the answer lands in `<runtime>/shot/phone-reply.json`. `phone_elements` answers here with the driver's list as JSON (the outline, the refs, a tap by `ref` and `phone_wait` are trek-mcp's, which an agent's calls go through).

The driver by hand (what Trek does): `ios/scripts/install-device.sh` (or `ios/scripts/build-driver.sh <signed Trek.app> <dir>`), then `xcodebuild test-without-building -xctestrun <copy with TREK_DRIVER=1, TREK_DRIVER_PORT, TREK_DRIVER_TOKEN> -destination id=<udid> -only-testing:TrekDriver/Drive` and HTTP to port 8117 over usbmuxd: `GET /status` (screen in points, `version` 3 and `features` `path`, `keys`, `stream`, `elements`, and `stream`: who watches, how frames are taken and how long one takes), `POST /tap {x,y,hold_ms}`, `/swipe {x1,y1,x2,y2,duration_ms}`, `/path {points: [{x,y,t_ms}]}` (a touch that follows the points, `t_ms` from the touch-down and rising, resting where two points share a place; thinned to 34 ms between points, which iOS 27's synthesizer needs: at a mouse's 16 ms it moved nothing), `/type {text}`, `/key {key: home|lock|volume_up|volume_down|spotlight|enter|backspace|tab|escape|up|down|left|right|…, count}` (the arrows as a hardware keyboard's keys), `/open {bundle}`, `GET /screenshot` (`?w=<max width in pixels>&q=<JPEG quality>`; PNG without `q`; `&via=daemon` takes it as the stream does, to compare), `GET /stream` and `GET /elements` (above), `POST /stop`, each with `X-Trek-Token`. Its HTTP parsing, the stream's rules and the element list have their own tests: `xcodebuild test -project ios/TrekDriver/TrekDriver.xcodeproj -scheme TrekDriver -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17'`.

The driver in a simulator (never on the phone), for the stream and the elements: it serves on the Mac's own loopback there, so curl reaches it. Boot a simulator without opening Simulator.app, then

```sh
TEST_RUNNER_TREK_DRIVER=1 TEST_RUNNER_TREK_DRIVER_PORT=8127 TEST_RUNNER_TREK_DRIVER_TOKEN=simtest TEST_RUNNER_TREK_DRIVER_IDLE=3600 \
  xcodebuild test -project ios/TrekDriver/TrekDriver.xcodeproj -scheme TrekDriver -destination "id=<udid>" -only-testing:TrekDriver/Drive CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO &
curl -s -H 'X-Trek-Token: simtest' http://127.0.0.1:8127/status
curl -s -H 'X-Trek-Token: simtest' http://127.0.0.1:8127/elements
curl -s -m 5 -H 'X-Trek-Token: simtest' 'http://127.0.0.1:8127/stream?w=0&q=70&fps=30' -o /tmp/stream.bin   # frames for 5 s
cargo run -p trek-phone --example driver_bench -- 127.0.0.1:8127 simtest      # frames a second and input to picture, both ways
TREK_DRIVER_SIM=127.0.0.1:8127 TREK_DRIVER_TOKEN=simtest cargo test -p trek-mcp a_simulators_driver -- --ignored --nocapture   # the outline, a tap by ref, a wait
curl -s -X POST -H 'X-Trek-Token: simtest' http://127.0.0.1:8127/stop
```

Which iPhones usbmuxd sees and how (USB, or Wi-Fi with its address and the Mac's there), read only:

```sh
cargo run -p trek-phone --example devices
```

Why iPhone Mirroring can't reach the phone, from the Mac's side (whether Apple offers it where the Mac is, a management profile, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Handoff, Internet Sharing, firewall, VPN, the phone on the Mac's network, and how its last Connect ended in its log: not found, dropped, not set up, or turned down for the iPhone's region), with the one blocker last and the advice for an agent under it (how it all works and what the logs say: `docs/IPHONE-MIRRORING.md`):

```sh
cargo run -p trek-phone --example mirror -- diagnose
```

The tool runs the same checks when iPhone Mirroring times out with the phone locked, and its action line names that blocker with **Check again**.

## Code

`crates/trek-app/src/panels/phone.rs` (the tool, its agent calls, the status bar clock), `crates/trek-app/src/panels/phone/touch.rs` (the user's own mouse, scrolling and keys, the touch dot and trail), `crates/trek-phone/src/gesture.rs` (a press recorded into a tap, hold or path; scrolls into swipes; keys; each route's requests for them), `crates/trek-phone/src/route.rs` (the route and the strip's words, shared with `phone_status`), `crates/trek-phone/src/driver.rs` (starting and talking to Trek Driver, tools onto its endpoints, pixels to points, reading its stream, an unanswered action as unknown), `crates/trek-mcp/src/phone.rs` (the driver's list read into the outline's tree, a tap by ref, `phone_wait`), `crates/trek-mcp/src/outline.rs` (the outline's format, refs, found-again checks, filter and cap, shared with computer use and the Simulator), `ios/TrekDriver/` (the driver; `Stream.swift` and `Capture.swift` for the pushed picture, `Elements.swift` for the element list; `project.yml` for xcodegen), `ios/scripts/build-driver.sh` (puts it in Trek.app), `crates/trek-phone/src/mirror.rs` (iPhone Mirroring's states, read through Accessibility; Trek's Connect retries), `crates/trek-phone/src/diagnose.rs` (why it can't reach the phone), `crates/trek-phone/helper/main.swift` (USB capture, Bluetooth, iPhone Mirroring input), `crates/trek-phone/src/device.rs` (lockdown, AssistiveTouch, apps, files, the phone's clock, a TCP port on the phone, the link: USB or Wi-Fi), `crates/trek-ipc/src/phone.rs` (the tools).

## Verify

The strip names one route and never shows the Bluetooth pairing step when iPhone Mirroring is the route; an unlocked phone with the driver taps over USB and a locked one goes to iPhone Mirroring; Trek's iPhone app on the phone is never replaced by an older driver build (a build mismatch says to run install-device.sh again); `phone_status` says the same words; errors from `phone_tap`, `phone_swipe` and `phone_type` start with the one thing to do. A blank iPhone Mirroring window (it is blank for about a second between its own screens) isn't taken for the phone's screen. Unlocking the phone brings the picture back over USB by itself. AssistiveTouch is never switched on the Mirroring route. With no cable and the driver installed, an unlocked phone taps and shows its picture over Wi-Fi through the driver (the header says Wi-Fi), a locked one goes to iPhone Mirroring, plugging the cable in moves the route back to USB, and the screen never shows a stale picture as live over Wi-Fi. A click taps, a held click long-presses, a drag plays with its pauses (straight on a version 1 driver), a scroll scrolls the same way the Mac's would, typing after a click reaches a focused field and Escape gives the keys back; Trek's ⌘-shortcuts keep working while the picture has the keys. With a version 3 driver the picture follows a swipe as it happens (frames come pushed, and `/status` counts them under `stream`), it goes on with a version 2 driver (screenshots, as before), it comes back by itself after the phone was locked and unlocked or the cable pulled and put back, and the stream has no watcher a second or two after the tool is hidden with no agent holding the phone (`"clients": 0`). `phone_elements` lists the Home Screen's icons and an app's controls with refs, a permission alert's buttons included; `phone_tap` with a ref from it opens what it names; a ref from a screen that has gone is refused with "Read the elements again (phone_elements)" and nothing is tapped; the outline's lines read like `sim_elements`' (`[frame=x,y,width,height]`, never the old `(x,y w×h)` boxes); `phone_status` says in its Elements line whether they're to be had.

Known: XCTest's synthesized touches (the driver's `/path` and XCTest's own press-then-drag alike) open an icon's long-press menu but don't pick the icon up on the iOS 27 simulator's Home Screen (the drag pulls down Search instead, and once SpringBoard stayed that way through later taps until the simulator restarted); scrolling and paging follow the path. Not tried on a phone with the version 2 driver yet.

Known, of version 3: the stream and the elements ran only in the iOS 27 simulator; on a real iPhone the direct JPEG (and its fall-back), the frame rate over the cable and over Wi-Fi, and the stream through usbmuxd are untried. Element frames inside a Home Screen widget come from XCTest relative to the widget, so their boxes are off; a notification banner over an app isn't listed (SpringBoard isn't active then); an app held in landscape reports frames in its own orientation, while taps go in portrait points. The scroll indicators are recognised by their English labels.
